Engineering notebook / Process note

Why part orientation matters

A pocket that fills easily may also retain liquid. The orientation of a workpiece can change access, drainage and contact with the basket.

Reading noteProcess note · Industrial workpiece planning. Use these questions to prepare your engineering enquiry.

Trace a path into the feature

Use a sketch or a pair of photographs to show which direction a recess opens. Mark any narrow passages or intersections so they can be reviewed separately from the outside surfaces.

Trace the path back out

Sketch what happens when the basket leaves the bath. Look for pockets that point upward and surfaces that may carry liquid into the next stage. Include the resting position during transfer as well as the cleaning position.

Check the fixture as part of the trial

Record contact areas and spacing between parts. If the orientation changes during a trial, record the new layout and use the same inspection conditions for both observations.

Show the next production step

Where a clean component goes next influences how it should leave the line. A part going to coating, assembly or storage may need different checks. Let the next operation define what a useful result looks like.
