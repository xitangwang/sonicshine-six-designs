Illustrative project / Not a customer installation

Keep the surfaces apart.

A worked project example: a batch of turned shafts shares one basket. The trial must distinguish residue removal from contact marks caused by loading.

Illustrative project image
Design studyIllustrative sequence. This example does not report customer results, an installed machine or a validated process.

The demonstration brief

Imagine turned steel shafts with finished bearing surfaces and residual machining oil. The receiving team wants clean parts that can move to inspection without rubbing against one another.

A proposed trial sequence

  1. Map the contact points

    Show where a shaft may rest, and mark finished areas that must not touch the basket or another part.

  2. Define a representative load

    Photograph the layout and record the batch size and loaded mass. Leave enough information to reproduce the arrangement.

  3. Check the whole batch

    Select positions from more than one part of the basket. Compare residue, surface condition and drainage with the agreed reference.

  4. Review the handoff

    Discuss how parts leave the basket and when inspection occurs. A clean stage alone does not resolve marks from later handling.

What remains open

The fixture material, number of parts per basket and process sequence all need review. The trial plan would define how to compare possible layouts before a production configuration is proposed.
