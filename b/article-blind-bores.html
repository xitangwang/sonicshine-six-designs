Why access, orientation and an inspection plan matter when passages cannot be seen.

A clean face can hide a difficult passage

An internal passage does not have the same access or drainage as an open face. Start with a drawing or marked-up photo. Identify blind ends, intersecting bores and the orientation in which the part will be immersed or drained.

Look at the soil before the machine

Cutting fluid and loose metal fragments pose different inspection questions. Record which surfaces and passages matter most, and how the workpiece is handled between machining and cleaning.

Plan the inspection before the trial

Decide what evidence will show that the passage meets the next process requirement. The accepted method may differ between parts and sites; define it with the buyer before comparing trial options.
