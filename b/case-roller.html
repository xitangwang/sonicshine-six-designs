01 / Observation

Start with the obstruction.

Engraved roller cells can retain dried ink. A proposed cleaning route must account for the roller surface, ink chemistry and handling limits.

Record the coating, the ink system and any restrictions on chemistry or handling before discussing a trial.

Interactive process study

Follow the route.

Explore four planning steps. This is an illustration, not a validated process recipe.

Stage 01 / 04

Orient the workpiece

Record the coating, the ink system and any restrictions on chemistry or handling before discussing a trial.

Ask: Can liquid reach the surfaces that matter?

Stage 02 / 04

Review a trial recipe

Use a representative engraved roller to evaluate a proposed route for dried ink. Materials, chemistry and operating settings need confirmation.

Ask: What constraints rule out unsuitable settings or chemistry?

Stage 03 / 04

Control what leaves the bath

Consider how retained liquid will drain, how rinsing will be evaluated and whether drying is needed before the next operation.

Ask: What carryover or retained liquid would be unacceptable?

Stage 04 / 04

Agree the release check

Agree on a comparison method for cell condition and ink residue; do not infer clean cells from appearance alone.

Ask: Who reviews the comparison and decides what to change?

03 / Trial preparation

What to send.

Include photographs showing scale, the material and surface finish, the residue as it arrives, and the expected condition before the next operation. A representative sample is preferable to a specially cleaned part.
